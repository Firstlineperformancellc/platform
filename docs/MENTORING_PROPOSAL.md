# FLP Mentoring: Competitor Research and a Proposal

**Prepared for:** Scott Maslowe, for discussion before anything is built
**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Status:** PROPOSAL. Nothing in this document has been implemented.

---

## 1. What the market sells today

Nine services read in full, from the closest comparables to the hockey specialists.

| Service | Who mentors | Format | Price | What they don't do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Athlete To Athlete | NCAA D1 athletes, 30+ sports | 1-to-1 video call, weekly or biweekly, 30/45/60 min by age tier. Staff match you to a mentor within 48 hours. First call free. | $49–59 per session, or $139/month. Cancel early and owe half of unused sessions. | No film. No parent-visible record of what was said. Nothing hockey-specific. Safety and recording policy not published. |
| Athlete Mentorships | Two pros (NHL scout, PWHL forward) | 2-month cohort, weekly live group sessions, WhatsApp group, weekly assignments | $499 for the program | Group only. Fixed calendar. No 1-to-1. WhatsApp with minors. |
| Athlete Experience | Christian college and pro athletes | 40–45 min 1-to-1 video, family picks the schedule, staff matching | Not published | Faith-specific by design. Generic sport guidance. |
| 90TEN Hockey IQ | One coach plus staff | Weekly 25–30 min group Zoom, clip analysis of NHL/NCAA film, archived content | $50/month group; $250/month small group with personal game analysis | Not a marketplace. One coach's system. The "personal analysis" tier is the closest thing to FLP's breakdown, at a subscription. |
| Pro Ambitions | Former NHL player's camp brand | Monthly 45-min call on nutrition, mental game, off-ice | From $199/month | One call a month. Camp upsell vehicle. |
| Wheelhouse Hockey | Coaches | Personal coach by Zoom, text, or phone; video analysis | Analysis from $75 | Text access with minors. No marketplace. |
| Fortis Academy | Coaches | 3 hours of film reviewed per month plus three 30-min meetings | Subscription, price on request | Coach-led, not athlete-led. |
| CoachUp | Any coach who signs up | In-person and virtual lessons, coach sets price and packages | Coach-set, plus a one-time $24.99 placement fee | Quality varies wildly. No safeguarding model for video. |
| VOICEINSPORT | 50 college athletes (2026 launch) | 1-to-1, group sessions, and short video answers to questions, in-app | Not published | Girls and women in sport, all sports. Newest and best-designed of the set. |

**What the whole market has in common.** Every one of them sells conversation. The mentor and the kid talk about confidence, goals, nutrition, "the mental game." It's useful, and parents pay for it, but nothing anchors the call to what the player actually did on the ice last Saturday. Athlete To Athlete has raised venture money on this exact model, so the model works commercially. It just isn't differentiated.

**What none of them have.** The player's own game film, already uploaded, already reviewed by the same mentor, sitting right there. FLP has it as a by-product of the breakdown business. That is the whole opportunity.

## 2. The proposal in one paragraph

Make every FLP mentoring session a **Film Room**: a recorded live video call where the mentor and the youth athlete watch the athlete's own film together and talk through it in real time. The session starts from the mentor's own worksheet, ends with the mentor updating that worksheet, and the sequence of breakdowns and sessions becomes a single running **Development Log** the parent can read from the first order onward. Competitors sell a chat. FLP sells a documented development arc with the player's own footage as the evidence.

## 3. How it works, step by step

**Where a session comes from.** Two doors, as Alex described:

- **From a breakdown.** When the mentor delivers, the viewing page offers "Go through this live with your FLP Mentor," pre-priced, one tap to see the mentor's open slots. This is the primary door. The parent has just watched a stranger say smart things about their kid; the impulse to book is strongest right then.
- **From the marketplace.** Every mentor profile shows session prices and a "Book a Film Room" button. Parents can book without a prior breakdown, and the intake asks them to upload a clip or pick one already on file, so the session still has film to work from.

**Before the session.** The mentor's prep screen shows the youth athlete's profile, the last worksheet, the parent's one-line note on what to cover, and the film cued to the first clip the mentor flagged. Five minutes of prep, no hunting.

**In the session.** A recorded video call inside FLP. The mentor shares the film, pauses, points, rewinds, asks "what did you see here?" The kid answers. That exchange is the product.

**After the session.** Within 24 hours the mentor fills in a short recap: three takeaways, one or two drills, one next step. It appends to the Development Log and the parent gets an email. The parent rates the session, same 1-to-5 with a testimonial, same gate at 3 stars.

**Continuity.** The same mentor by default. Rebooking is one tap from the log. If the mentor is unavailable, the parent gets the same wait-or-second-choice prompt as breakdowns.

## 4. Session formats and pricing

Prices follow the breakdown tiers and the same splits: 60/40 for NCAA and PWHL, 70/30 for Pro.

| Format | Length | NCAA | PWHL | Pro | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Film Room** | 30 min | $89 | $119 | $349 | The standard session. One game, three or four situations, one drill to leave with. |
| **Film Room, extended** | 60 min | $149 | $199 | $599 | Full-game walk-through, or a goalie session, which needs the time. |
| **Breakdown add-on** | 30 min, booked from a delivered breakdown within 14 days | $69 | $99 | $299 | The upsell. Priced below the standalone session because the mentor has already done the film work. |
| **Season Arc** | Four 30-min sessions, used within 8 weeks | $299 | $399 | $1,199 | The subscription without the subscription. Roughly 15% off, prepaid, ties a family to one mentor for two months. |

Why these numbers. Athlete To Athlete charges $49–59 for a D1 athlete's time with no film. FLP's NCAA session at $89 includes the film, the recording, and the written recap, which is a defensible premium, not a stretch. The Pro tier follows the $1,000 breakdown: a parent paying that for a review will pay a third of it to talk to the same player for half an hour.

A mentor at the NCAA tier earns $53 for a 30-minute session and $89 for the extended one. That is well above what they'd make coaching a camp, which matters for supply.

**Not at launch, worth planning for:** Position Rooms. Small-group 45-minute sessions, four seats, one position, one mentor. At $39 a seat for NCAA the mentor makes $94 an hour and a family gets in the door for the price of a pizza. It's the natural funnel into 1-to-1, and it's what 90TEN and VOICEINSPORT are doing at the group level. Group scheduling is real work, so it's Phase 2.

## 5. Scheduling and the rules

- **Availability.** Mentors set recurring weekly windows, say Tuesday and Thursday 7 to 9 pm, plus a "sessions on deck" cap like breakdowns. The marketplace only shows slots inside those windows.
- **Booking.** Parent picks a slot, pays at booking, gets a calendar invite. Reminders at 24 hours and 1 hour to both sides, email now and push once the apps ship.
- **Rescheduling and cancellation.** Free up to 24 hours before. Inside 24 hours, the parent forfeits the session; a mentor who cancels inside 24 hours triggers a full refund and a mark on their scorecard.
- **No-shows.** Ten-minute grace. Youth athlete no-show: charged, one courtesy rebook per family. Mentor no-show: full refund, scorecard mark, second one triggers an admin review.
- **Time zones.** Everything displayed in the viewer's local time, stored in UTC. Michigan families and a mentor in Sweden should never do arithmetic.

## 6. Safeguarding, built in, not bolted on

This is where FLP beats every competitor in the table, and it costs almost nothing extra because the contract already requires most of it.

- **The parent books, the parent can join.** Every session is on the parent's account, and the join link works for the parent at any time. A "parent present" toggle at booking lets a family require it.
- **Always recorded, no exceptions.** Recording is locked on at the call-provider level. Both sides see a "recorded for FLP review" notice when they join. Recordings are stored in FLP-owned storage for 90 days, visible to admin, then deleted on a schedule. Alex and Bryan can watch any session from the admin panel.
- **No side channels.** No chat, no messaging, no exchange of phone numbers or socials inside FLP. Every touch between a mentor and a family goes through a session or a delivered breakdown, both of which leave a record. This is the single biggest difference from the WhatsApp and text-access competitors.
- **Written record after every session.** The recap is a mentor obligation, not an option. A session with no recap after 24 hours flags on the scorecard.
- **Mentor conduct policy** signed at approval, with the Quality Control Audit as the parent's escalation path for sessions as well as breakdowns.

## 7. What's in it for each side

**Parents** get a coach who has been where their kid wants to go, looking at their kid's actual game, with a written record they can hand to the next coach. They can watch the recording. They can be in the room. Nothing about it is a stranger texting their child.

**Youth athletes** get the thing every young player craves and almost never gets: an athlete they look up to telling them, clip by clip, what to do differently, and then remembering next time.

**Mentors** get the highest hourly rate in this market for a session that mostly runs itself, because the film provides the agenda. Prep is five minutes. The recap is five minutes. Their profile shows their session rating and turnaround right next to their breakdown stats.

**FLP** gets a second revenue line from customers it already has, at zero acquisition cost, with a safeguarding story it can put in writing when a rink or association asks. And every session generates more worksheet data, which makes the Development Log more valuable, which makes the next breakdown more likely.

## 8. What this means for the build

**Inside the contract, Milestone 2:** booking and availability, the recorded call with the parent join link, reminders, the mentor's recap form, the Development Log view, the add-on offer on the breakdown page, and the QCA path for sessions. The call itself uses the provider's ready-made call screen with screen sharing, so the mentor shares the film from their own window. This is the "mentor screen-shares the film" version of the Film Room and it works on day one with nothing custom.

**After launch, when it earns its keep:** a synchronized player inside the call, where both sides see the same frame and the mentor's pointer draws on it. That's the polished Film Room. It's a bounded piece of work, but it's not in the thirteen items and I wouldn't spend Milestone 2 on it.

**Phase 2 candidates:** Position Rooms, a monthly subscription that bundles one breakdown and two sessions, and the ability for a mentor to attach a short clip to a recap.
