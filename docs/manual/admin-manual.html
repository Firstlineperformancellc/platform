Section 1

How the platform fits together

First Line Performance is a marketplace. Families buy video breakdowns and live Film Room sessions from vetted mentors. The platform takes the money, routes the work, delivers the result, and pays the mentor. The admin panel is where you steer all of it.

The people

  • Parents own every family account. The youth athlete is a profile under the parent, never a login. Parents choose mentors, upload film, pay, rate, file audits, and may join any Film Room.
  • Mentors are approved athletes with a public profile, a level set by you, and a share of each sale. They accept requests, record breakdowns with their own tools, fill in the Player Development Worksheet, run Film Rooms, and are paid through Stripe. Everything they're told is in the Mentor FLP Service User Manual, linked from your Control center.
  • Admins are you. Admins can read everything and act on everything; every admin action is written to an audit log with who did it and when.

The two products

  • Breakdown. Parent picks a mentor and pays → the request is offered to the mentor → the mentor accepts, records, and delivers a video plus a worksheet PDF → the parent rates it and may file a Quality Control Audit within 7 days → the mentor's payout is released.
  • Film Room. Parent books a 30- or 60-minute recorded live session inside the mentor's weekly windows and pays at booking → the mentor confirms → both join, recording locked on → the mentor files a recap → payout.

Money

FLP is the merchant of record. Parents pay FLP through Stripe Checkout; mentors are paid by a Stripe transfer to their own Stripe Express account; FLP never holds money outside Stripe. Each level sets the price and the mentor's share. Section 9 covers the ledger.

Your first day: signing in, the Control center, admin seats

Signing in

Go to app.firstlineperform.com and sign in with your admin email and password. Admin accounts are ordinary accounts that have been promoted (see "Admin seats" below). Forgot password on the sign-in page sends a reset link. Signing out on one device no longer signs you out everywhere. If the panel ever says "Your session has ended", sign in again; nothing was lost.

The tabs

Overview · Users · Mentors · Levels & tiles · Orders & jobs · Film Room · Audits · Ledger · Support · Health · Settings · Admin User Manual. The panel works on a phone; the tabs wrap.

Admin seats

Under Settings, Admin seats lists every admin and lets you add one by email with Make admin. The person signs up as a parent first; then their account is promoted here. An email listed before the person signs up becomes an admin the moment they do. Admins can't be suspended or deleted from Users; manage them from Settings.

A daily routine that works

  1. Open the Control center. Anything gold needs you.
  2. Mentor applications: approve with a level, or decline.
  3. Jobs needing a mentor: assign anything unassigned.
  4. Open audits: read, decide, close.
  5. Support desk: answer the inbox.
  6. Payouts owed: pay what's past the quality window, or let the Friday schedule or Automatic do it (Section 9).
  7. Glance at Health. Green means leave it alone.
Section 3

Users: every account, suspend, delete, contact

The Users tab lists every account. Search by name or email; filter with All, Parents, Mentors, Admins, or Suspended / deleted.

What each row shows

  • Name, email, phone and mailing address (if they've given them), role, and a status pill: Active, Unconfirmed (never clicked their confirmation email), Suspended, or Deleted.
  • When they joined and last signed in.
  • For parents: their youth athletes, orders, Film Rooms, and money spent and refunded. For mentors: jobs, sessions, and money earned and paid.

Open on a row shows the activity timeline: account created, sign-ins, orders placed, film uploaded, breakdowns accepted and delivered, reviews, audits, Film Rooms booked and completed, packs, payouts, and every admin action taken on the account.

Contact

Contact opens a composer. The message goes out by email from support@firstlineperform.com and opens a support ticket assigned to you, marked "waiting on customer", so their reply lands in the Support desk under the same number. If the mail provider refuses the address, the panel says so.

Suspend and unsuspend

Suspend blocks sign-in at once and emails the person the reason you type (optional). A suspended mentor comes off the marketplace. Unsuspend restores everything and emails them. Both are logged.

Delete

Delete always asks "Are you sure you want to delete this user?" first.

  • A parent with no orders, sessions, or payouts is erased outright. One with history is deactivated instead, so ledgers and other people's records keep their meaning.
  • A mentor goes through the mentor delete flow (Section 4): identity wiped, email freed, a full record kept under Mentors → Deleted, restorable.

You can't act on your own account, and admins are managed from Settings.

Section 4

Mentors: applications, approval, tiers, deletion and restore

The Mentors tab opens on Applications whenever any are waiting, otherwise on Approved. The filters are Applications, Approved, Suspended, Deactivated, All, and Deleted, each with a count. The team is also emailed at team@firstlineperform.com within minutes of every new application, with the applicant's details and a link here.

The mentor card

  • Name, email, positions, team, and highest level played; status pill, level pill, and Verified or Unverified.
  • Their bio; any special circumstances they wrote, in amber; age and gender; why they want to mentor; their Elite Prospects link.
  • The contractor agreement line: signed on which date, as which name, from which state or province, with a guardian for minors, and a link to the agreement. Applicants from before signing was part of the form show "No signed contractor agreement on file" in amber.
  • For anyone past the application stage, the scorecard: completed, on deck against capacity, average turnaround, on-time rate, rating and count, offers accepted / declined / expired, audits, Film Rooms completed, no-shows / late cancels / declines, last delivered. The scorecard is admin-only; families see rating, count, turnaround, and completed jobs.

Approving an application

  1. Read the application. Check the Elite Prospects page if they gave one, and the special circumstances if any.
  2. Pick a Tier on the card. The panel suggests one from their highest level played. Approval without a level is refused, because the marketplace only lists levelled mentors.
  3. Press Approve. The mentor is emailed, their profile goes live, and they start at the level's default jobs on deck. Or press Decline: the application is deactivated and the person is not emailed.

Managing an approved mentor

  • Mark verified records that you checked their credentials. It isn't shown publicly.
  • Suspend takes them off the marketplace and blocks sign-in; Unsuspend reverses it. Deactivate & block is the firmer version; Reactivate brings them back.
  • Tier. Every card past the application stage has a Tier dropdown. Picking a new one asks you to confirm: new orders and Film Rooms use the new level's prices and share, and anything already booked keeps the price it was paid at. The card then shows the last change, the date, and who made it. Only admins can change a mentor's tier; the mentor can't.

Deleting a mentor so they can start over

Delete is on every card. It asks "Are you sure you want to delete this user?", with an optional reason for the record, and then:

  • Wipes the live account: name, email, phone, and address come off, the profile disappears from the marketplace, and sign-in is blocked. The platform never emails a deleted account.
  • Frees the email, so the same person can apply again from scratch with the same address.
  • Keeps everything in a record under the Deleted filter: who, when, why, and the full profile and application as they stood.

Old orders, payouts and reviews stay attached to the wiped account, so history is intact. A mentor with breakdowns in progress, open requests, or upcoming Film Rooms can't be deleted until that work is finished or reassigned; the panel tells you what's open.

Restore on a deleted record puts the mentor back exactly as they were, as long as their email hasn't signed up again. If it has, the panel says so: keep the new account, or delete it first and then restore the old one.

Section 6

Orders & jobs: the breakdown pipeline and refunds

How a breakdown moves

  1. Paid. The parent picks a mentor, uploads film or a YouTube link, names focus areas, optionally a second choice, and pays. Nothing is offered until the payment lands.
  2. Offered to the first choice, who has 48 hours to accept (nudged at 24). Decline or expiry → the second choice.
  3. Waiting. If the first choice is at capacity, the job waits the number of days the parent chose before falling through to the second choice.
  4. Unassigned. Nobody took it. It lands in your queue.
  5. Accepted. The turnaround clock starts (72 hours by default, or the level's own). Reminders go to the mentor at 24 and 48 hours.
  6. Delivered, on time or late. The parent gets the video and the worksheet PDF, can rate it, and has 7 days to file an audit. The mentor's payout is created.

The Orders & jobs tab

Filters: Needs attention (unassigned, waiting, offered, or accepted with under 24 hours left), Active, Delivered, All. Each card shows the athlete and parent, focus areas, the status, who holds it ("Offered to X (choice 1), expires …", "Waiting 3 days for first choice since …", or the mentor), hours left, and for delivered jobs On time or Late.

Actions

  • Assign / Reassign: pick a mentor from the dropdown and press it. The job is pinned to that mentor; use it for unassigned jobs, for a family that writes in, or to move a job off a mentor who can't finish it.
  • Extend 24h: adds a day to an accepted job's deadline. Logged; use it when the delay is the family's (film arrived late) or agreed.
  • Refund order: type a reason (emailed to the parent) and an amount, or leave the amount blank for everything not yet refunded. The money goes back to the parent's card through Stripe. A full refund closes the order and voids the mentor's unpaid payout. A payout that already went out is not pulled back; the panel tells you the amount and you decide. An order from free-preview mode was never charged, so a refund there only records the fact.
Refunds made in the Stripe dashboard flow back into the platform on their own, so either place works. The panel is the better record.
Section 7

Film Room: sessions, no-shows, recordings

The rules, as the platform enforces them

  • Parents book 30- or 60-minute slots inside a mentor's weekly windows, at least 12 hours ahead and up to 14 days out, and pay at booking. Marketplace bookings must attach film.
  • The mentor confirms within 24 hours, or the booking is cancelled and the family refunded automatically.
  • Free cancellation up to 24 hours before. Inside that window the parent forfeits and the mentor is still paid; a mentor cancelling inside it is logged against their scorecard and the family refunded.
  • Recording starts when the mentor joins and cannot be stopped by anyone; both sides are told. Recordings are kept 90 days.
  • 10-minute grace. A family that hasn't appeared by then can be marked a no-show by the mentor (mentor paid). A mentor who never joins becomes a mentor no-show a day after the booked end: full refund, scorecard mark, you're emailed.
  • Sessions nobody closes complete themselves after the booked end. The mentor's recap is due within 24 hours and lands in the family's Development Log.
  • Add-on: a 30-minute session a parent can book within 14 days of a delivered breakdown. Season Arc: four 30-minute sessions over eight weeks, booked one at a time from the pack. Prices per level.

The Film Room tab

Filters: Needs attention (live past its end, completed without a recap, or any no-show), Upcoming, Completed, All. Each card shows the athlete, the mentor, the format, the time, the parent's note, the recap or "Recap not filed yet", any cancel reason, and the recordings.

Actions

  • Cancel + refund with a reason that goes to the parent.
  • Mentor no-show: refunds the family and marks the mentor's scorecard. Family no-show: the mentor is paid.
  • Mark completed: closes a session that got stuck live.
  • Open recording: a one-hour link to watch it, for audits and safety reviews.
Section 8

Audits and reviews

Quality Control Audits

A parent may file an audit within 7 days of a delivered breakdown or a completed Film Room. It appears under Audits with the parent, the mentor, the job or session, the reason, and when it was filed. The mentor's payout for that work goes on hold the moment an audit opens.

  1. Watch the breakdown or recording and read the worksheet. Add note as you go; notes are internal.
  2. Choose Close with: Dismiss (the work stands; payout released), Partial refund (type the dollars), Full refund (payout voided), Reassign to another mentor (pick one; the job is re-offered), or Other.
  3. Press Close audit. The resolution is recorded and the parent is emailed. Refunds go through Stripe like any other.

Reviews to moderate

Parents rate breakdowns and Film Rooms from 1 to 5. Ratings at or above the threshold in Settings (4 by default) publish to the mentor's public profile on their own. Anything below waits here: Publish or Hide. Hidden reviews still count on the admin scorecard.

Section 9

The payout ledger and Stripe

How a payout comes to exist

When a mentor delivers a breakdown or a Film Room completes, a payout row is created for their share, in status Owed. It is Held while an audit or a card dispute on that sale is open. It becomes Paid when the money is sent, Voided if the sale is refunded first, and Failed if Stripe refused it.

The Ledger tab

Totals for Owed, Held, and Paid at the top; filters for each status; a by-mentor subtotal for the view; then every payout with the mentor, what it was for and for whom, the dates, and how it was paid ("via Stripe" or "manually"). A held row shows its reason; an owed row whose last attempt failed shows why.

Actions

  • Pay via Stripe: a transfer to the mentor's Stripe Express account, tied to the charge that funded it, so it can never exceed what the parent paid and doesn't wait for FLP's balance to settle. Needs the mentor to have finished payout setup; the row says if they haven't.
  • Mark paid manually: records a payment you made outside Stripe.
  • Hold with a reason, and Release.

Manual or automatic

Under Settings, Mentor payouts is Manual (you press Pay), Automatic after the waiting period, or Every other Friday (a scheduled batch at 9:00 Eastern that sends everything past the waiting period and emails you a summary; the Ledger shows the next run and a button to run it now). On Automatic, each owed payout is sent once it is older than the waiting period; blank uses the audit window, so a parent's audit can still stop it. Held payouts are never sent automatically. A real failure emails the admins once and is retried every six hours. A mentor who hasn't finished Stripe setup, or a sale that was never charged (free preview), stays owed with the reason on the row and no alarm.

Stripe, in one paragraph

Parents pay FLP through Stripe Checkout. Mentors onboard to Stripe Express from their profile ("Set up payouts"), where Stripe collects identity, bank, and tax details; FLP never sees them. Stripe tells the platform when a payment lands, when a checkout is abandoned, when a refund is made, when a card is disputed (the mentor's payout is held and you're emailed), and when a mentor's account is verified. FLP's own Stripe dashboard, under FLP's Stripe login, is where FLP's money, fees, and disputes are managed; answer disputes there, then release or void the payout here.

Payments mode. Settings → Payments has a Free preview mode in which orders and bookings complete with no charge, for demos and beta testing. A red banner shows in the panel while it's on. Switch it back to Stripe before real families use the platform.
Section 10

Support desk

Three doors, one queue

  • Email to support@firstlineperform.com. A small script in that mailbox hands every new message to the platform within five minutes. Customer replies land on the same ticket by the [FLP-123] tag in the subject.
  • In the app: parents and mentors have a Support link on their dashboard.
  • The website: the contact page posts straight into the queue.

New tickets and customer replies email every admin.

Working a ticket

The Support tab shows Inbox (waiting on FLP), Waiting on customer, Resolved, and All, with search. Open a ticket:

  • Reply is a real email to the requester from FLP Support, threaded under their message. Pick After sending: Wait for their answer (moves it out of the inbox until they write back; it returns on its own), Mark resolved, or Keep open.
  • Insert a canned reply drops in a saved answer. Manage them under Canned replies: title, body, sort order.
  • Internal note is never emailed; use it to hand a ticket to someone with context.
  • Status, Priority (Low, Normal, High, Urgent), Assigned to, and Tags (free text: billing, film room, bug, mentor).
  • Open in Users jumps to the requester's account.
Answer from the ticket, not from Gmail, so the thread and the record stay in one place. The Mail setup page under Support explains the mailbox script; the Health tab watches that it's alive.

Section 12

Service health

Once an hour the platform sends one small, read-only request to each connected service and records the answer. The Health tab shows each service, the last 48 checks, and a Run button to check now. The badge on the Overview is the summary.

StatusMeaning
HealthyAnswered normally.
Needs attentionAnswered, but something about the setup is incomplete. The detail says what.
UnhealthyDid not answer or refused. Every admin is emailed when a service turns unhealthy and again when it recovers.
Not connectedNot set up yet; not counted.

The services: database, sign-in, file storage, video (Mux), calls (Daily), email (Resend), the support mailbox script's heartbeat, Stripe, the web app, the marketing site, and disk space on the server. If something is unhealthy for more than an hour, contact the lead developer with the service name and the detail text.
